Joining the lab

Our lab is part of the Departamento de Engenharia de Materiais at the Centro de Ciências Exatas e de Tecnologia, on the Universidade Federal de São Carlos in São Carlos, Brazil. This page summarizes the overall instructions to join the lab as a student or postdoc. If you have any doubts, feel free to contact us

Why are you joining the team?

If you are interested in joining the lab we should first start answering the question: why do you want to join the team?

This is a crucial question as it will help us to determine if we share similar goals and expectations. It is also important that you consider that we are an upcoming research group and that we are still in the process of structuring it. In the future, we aim to foster a tightly connected community of researchers and students that can help out each other and grow together.

To help you answer the question of why you want to join the team, you can check out our Expectations page so you can discover what are the commitments and responsibilities of both the lab members and Murilo.

Who we are looking for?

Although we are located in a materials science and engineering department, we are open to students from any programme and background that are familiar with calculus, physics and programming.

Our “digital lab space” is centered around our GitHub organization. That is where we will centralize our code, documentation and project management. Previous experience with programming, version control and GitHub is not mandatory, but team members will be required to learn and use these tools.

We are more than happy to help you learn these tools, but we expect that you also will be motivated to do so. We have a strong focus on open science and reproducibility, so without these skills it will be difficult to work in the lab.

Regarding AI, although we understand that it can be a powerful tool, we believe that it has an intrinsic “learning debt”. That means that whatever shortcuts that AI can provide, it can lead to a lack of understanding of the underlying concepts, which can have negative consequences. Thus, any use of AI should be disclosed and discussed with the lab members and Murilo. We are also working on a conduct code for AI use in the lab along with a set of guidelines for repositories containing AI generated code, which will be available soon.

Let’s talk

If you are curious about our work and feel that this sounds like an interesting adventure, please contact Murilo to check if he is able to supervise more students. To guarantee that every student and postdoc receives the care and support they need, there are only a limited number of positions available at any given time.

At the moment, as the lab is still being structured we are completely open to new members. Thus, if you are interested, simply reach out to Murilo explaining what activities you would like to pursue, your interests and your current professional plans, so we can look for the best way to integrate you into the lab.

What if you discover that you want to leave the team?

Every relationship has a beginning and an end. We hope that you will have a great time with us and become a future collaborator of the lab. However, we do understand that people and things can change and if you change your mind we will support you in your decision and try to help you to transition. However, it is important to consider that research projects, specially those funded by funding agencies such as FAPESP or CNPq, are long-term commitments, and it is not always simple to change their direction or to leave them. Thus, you should always feel free to share your concerns and doubts with Murilo and your future colleagues, so we can work together to find a solution that works for everyone.

Opportunities

There are many opportunities for students and postdocs at UFSCar, regarding both national and international applicants.

Undergraduate students

There are no restrictions on undergraduate students joining the lab. If we have open positions and if you are interested, let’s talk. There are many ways to be a member of the lab, from doing an undergraduate research project, to doing a bachelor thesis or to make an internship. You can look our research page, to see some of the projects that we are working.

For undergraduate students, we will always start with low risk volunteer projects, so you can get to know the lab and the research we do before applying to a scholarship. This is because scholarships require research outcomes that must be delivered.

Graduate students

Applications are handled by the Geophysics graduate program at IAG. However, please speak with Leo before applying if you’re interested in joining the lab. This is a requirement for PhD students and highly encouraged for MSc students. While MSc students can apply without having a supervisor, there is no guarantee that the lab will be able to accommodate you even if you are offered a place in the programme.

The programme has a good number of CAPES scholarships for both national and international students. There is no relocation funding so you’ll have to make your way to São Paulo using your own funds.

We can also apply for FAPESP scholarships, which pay significantly more but are harder to get. If this is something you want to do, then please let Leo know and we should apply as soon as possible (even before you are officially accepted into the graduate programme).

Postdocs

FAPESP has the best scholarships for postdocs and accepts national and international applicants. Their scholarships are very generous and are usually tax-free in Brazil. There are other scholarships available from time to time so it’s good to check with Leo if there is anything that would suite you.

There is no entry requirements or selection process in the department for postdocs. So it’s mostly a matter of finding funding for a scholarship. That said, postdocs are expected to be independent researchers and bring their own project ideas.
